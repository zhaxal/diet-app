## Design Specificity Verdict

The interface is authored for this product rather than category-interchangeable: tabular numerals, hairline panels, cool monochrome surfaces, an amber signal, and the grid-backed calorie readout make it feel like a daily instrument, not a generic wellness dashboard. The execution is especially strong in dark mode.

The product-specific visual language is not yet fully matched by the repeat-food loop. A known food still requires opening the composer, choosing it, then confirming or logging it. That is careful, but not yet the low-friction capture path promised by the product.

## Overall Impression

This is an unusually coherent operating UI with real respect for data integrity, offline use, and one-handed interaction. Its biggest opportunity is to distinguish deliberate confirmation from unnecessary routine friction: known, trusted repeats should feel instantaneous and recoverable.

## What's Working

- **Instrument-panel language:** `FoodTab`, `Meter`, and the global numeric treatment make measured quantities the visual content. Nothing looks like generic lifestyle marketing.
- **Trustworthy arithmetic:** `AddFood` keeps provenance, amount basis, compatible units, and a preview together so the user can see what will be recorded before logging.
- **Mobile operating rigor:** safe-area handling, a fixed bottom nav, 44px touch targets, focus-trapped dialogs, persistent drafts, offline status, retry, and Undo all support interrupted daily use.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The repeat-food path is not actually rapid

**Why it matters:** Favorites and Recent are reachable only after opening the composer. Selecting one opens a composed state rather than writing a known repeat, making the everyday path open → pick → confirm/log.

**Fix:** Place a compact, meal-aware quick-add strip directly beneath the dominant Log food surface once history exists. Let one tap log the last-known amount with Undo; reserve an explicit Adjust action for Confirm & Scale.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

### [P1] The empty state divides the primary action

**Why it matters:** Top-level `LOG FOOD` competes with `LOG FOOD TO SNACK`, while calorie and nutrient goal links lead toward the same setup. A hurried user has to infer which is canonical.

**Fix:** Retain the top logging surface as the only food CTA. Turn the lower empty state into concise status plus quick-add (where applicable), and collapse setup to one quiet `Targets unset — Set goals` affordance.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P2] A URL-supplied future date can lose its selected day in the week strip

**Why it matters:** The supplied mobile snapshot shows `WED, SEP 30` while the week strip displays Sep 6–12 with no active cell. The primary location signal then contradicts the reading.

**Fix:** Normalize future URLs to today or shift the week strip to include every selected date. Always render exactly one active day.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### [P2] Assistant provenance is too cryptic

**Why it matters:** A lower-case `ai` marker is hover-dependent and does not translate into a trustworthy, accessible source signal on a phone.

**Fix:** Use a restrained `SRC: AI` label with an accessible name such as `Logged by assistant`; keep it visually subordinate to the food itself.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P3] Refresh hides inside a timestamp

**Why it matters:** `0 entries · 09:15 PM ↻` looks like status, not a refresh control, so users may not discover how to verify a live day.

**Fix:** Separate the timestamp from a real, visible 44px refresh glyph control with a clear label.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

- **Alex, power user:** Keyboard shortcuts, batch support, and barcode scanning are strong, but a routine repeat cannot be completed in one action. The confirmation state will feel like repeated friction rather than safety.
- **Sam, accessibility-dependent user:** Dialog semantics, focus trapping, Escape, and focus restoration are excellent. The dashboard has weak semantic landmarks around readings, and the `ai` source marker is not a reliable accessible label.
- **Casey, distracted mobile user:** Drafts, offline recovery, and touch floors help. The primary action arrives high on the page and habitual foods sit behind another disclosure, so interruption is likely between selecting a repeat and saving it.

## Minor Observations

- Dark mode is particularly successful: the inverted logging action and amber signal remain intentional, not merely inverted.
- The manual-entry sheet starts sparse and understandable; do not solve fast logging by making it denser.
- Weight can push actual daily entries lower on small screens even though those entries are stronger evidence of the day.
- Trends are appropriately optional; their summary should remain subordinate to the daily reading.

## Questions to Consider

1. Should a trusted repeat log immediately with Undo, or does the product intentionally require confirmation for every repeat?
2. Should a deep-linked date always drive the selected week window, even when it lies in the future?
3. Would `SRC: AI` make assistant-written records feel more inspectable and correctable than the current tiny `ai` marker?
