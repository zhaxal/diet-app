# Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** The result feels authored rather than generic. The tabular numerals, faint calorie grid, hairline panels, inverted selected states, and single amber signal form a credible terminal-readout system. Its weak point is food specificity: nearly the same visual language could run a workout, finance, or home-telemetry product. The capture experience should carry more of the product's food-specific character, rather than relying principally on labels and meal groupings.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs --json app/page.tsx` exited 0 with `[]`: zero findings, rules, locations, or false positives. That is a clean mechanical result for the target file, not a blanket proof that the component tree has no interaction or UX issues.

**Visual overlays:** No reliable user-visible overlay is available. A fresh tab at port 3100 was refused; at port 3000 it stayed in `LOADING` after about ten seconds. The browser's security policy blocked mutable script injection, so the detector overlay, console evidence, and its live server were not started.

# Overall Impression

This is a confident, carefully built instrument panel with unusually good resilience. It makes checking a day feel precise and trustworthy. Its biggest missed opportunity is that the important action — adding food in a few seconds — looks and behaves too much like a secondary reporting panel.

# What's Working

- **A coherent visual language.** The restrained palette, flat rules, monospace figures, and deliberate use of amber make numbers feel like the product rather than decoration.
- **A sound reading order.** Time context, calories, nutrients, weight, then meals is logical for reviewing a day, and the snapshots show that hierarchy holding across phone and desktop.
- **Thoughtful recovery and input support.** Live status, offline labeling, drafts, Undo, unit compatibility, focus handling, and keyboard/accelerator paths make the data feel recoverable rather than fragile.

# Priority Issues

## [P1] Logging is visually subordinate to reporting

**Why it matters:** On a phone, the user scans date, calorie, macro, weight, and meal reporting before finding an `Add food` control that resembles the optional Trends accordion. For a product whose success is low-friction capture, that reverses the real job.

**Fix:** Make `Log food` a persistent, unmistakable thumb-zone primary action just above the bottom navigation. Keep the selected meal as compact context within that control, instead of asking users to resolve meal selection before they enter the capture flow.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

## [P1] Manual entry is a nutrition-data-model test

**Why it matters:** The mobile sheet combines name, four macros, amount, unit, meal, optional trace nutrients, direct-portion versus per-100 basis, saving, favoriting, and logging. A simple food turns into an accounting task, increasing mistakes and abandonment.

**Fix:** Start with food name, calories, selected meal, and one clear portion control. Put advanced nutrients and the label-basis model behind progressive disclosure, while retaining an explicit scaling preview for people who need exact control.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

## [P2] First-run activation presents competing starts

**Why it matters:** Goal setup repeats in multiple places, while Targets, Log Food, and AI Assistant appear as co-equal first moves. A newcomer cannot tell whether success means configuring a plan, recording a meal, or connecting an assistant.

**Fix:** Make the first successful food entry the activation path. Reduce goals to one secondary prompt and introduce the MCP/assistant route after the user has a day record to inspect.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable onboard`

## [P2] Date and meal choices consume capture bandwidth

**Why it matters:** The week strip, Today control, date field, four meals, quick-add sources, and manual-entry controls can create 5–8 simultaneous choices before a user logs anything.

**Fix:** Let the week strip own normal day navigation on phones; put the full date picker behind the date label. Surface meal selection as a lightweight step inside `Log food`, defaulting intelligently from time of day or the last choice.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

# Persona Red Flags

**Alex (Power User):** Recent items, barcode, copying, keyboard shortcuts, and MCP make logging fast once learned. But shortcut discovery is largely confined to first-run or Settings, and batch power ends at adding: correction of several already-logged entries is still one item at a time.

**Jordan (First-Timer):** `TDEE`, `MCP`, and `Quote per 100 g/ml` ask for domain knowledge before the UI explains why it matters. The three competing first-run routes and duplicate goal prompts obscure the safe first action: log one food.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User):** The bottom navigation, large touch floor, and retained drafts work well for interruptions. The action she needs is visually buried above a dense dashboard, and the manual sheet is too decision-heavy for a one-handed kitchen moment.

# Minor Observations

- Dark mode best expresses the terminal-readout identity; light mode is clean but more interchangeable.
- `Unlogged meals` risks reading as a compliance checklist rather than useful daily context.
- The unresolved browser `LOADING` state is insufficient evidence of a shipped defect, but loading must always have an observable exit, timeout, or recovery action.
- Secondary macro states should not rely primarily on red/green; accompany state color with an explicit textual condition.

# Questions to Consider

- If someone has ten seconds in a kitchen, should `Add food` ever resemble an optional report accordion?
- Is first-run success a configured nutrition plan, one trustworthy food entry, or an assistant connection — and why are all three currently presented equally?
- Could the assistant be framed as another input method inside a single logging action, rather than a competing destination?
