## Design Specificity Verdict

### LLM Assessment

**Highly specific and uncompromising.** The "Terminal Readout" aesthetic is fiercely tailored to this exact product. The rigid division of labour between tabular monospace (measured data) and system sans (authored text), the single-signal amber rule, the 1px hairline panelling, and the flat-by-doctrine elevation policy could not be lifted into any other product category without looking completely out of place. This is not a themed template; it is a bespoke instrument face built around one question: what did I eat today, and how does it compare to what I intended?

The system's specificity runs deeper than surface aesthetics. The unit-restatement logic in AddFood (switching 241g to 8.5oz rather than keeping "241" in the field), the ranked quick-add strip that answers a different question at 8am than at 7pm, the `staleSince` indicator acknowledging that an AI assistant may have written to the same record — these are product-level design decisions, not UI pattern choices. A different diet tracker could not adopt them without adopting the product's entire philosophy.

**Where specificity falters:** The auth screens (login/register) are the one place the system's voice thins out. The copy — "Review your day or keep logging" — is fine, but the page is a generic centered card with two fields. It could belong to any app. The terminal readout persona doesn't extend to the front door.

## Overall Impression

This is one of the most disciplined single-developer design systems I have encountered. The restraint is genuine — not minimalism as fashion, but minimalism as engineering: every decision about what not to include is justified and documented. The product knows exactly what it is.

The single biggest opportunity is **the onboarding cliff**. The app is superb for its owner — a competent, self-hosting technologist who built it for themselves. But the stated direction is "personal now, a handful of invited people later," and right now, the product's second user would need to read PRODUCT.md to understand what they are looking at. There is no bridge between "I have an account" and "I understand how to log a meal."

## What's Working

1. **The quick-add ranking engine.** [`lib/quick-add-rank.ts`](file:///Users/zhakhangiranuarbek/Documents/Projects/diet-app/lib/quick-add-rank.ts) scores foods by frequency weighted by meal-time share, decayed on a 14-day half-life with a floor. The strip answers a different question at breakfast than at dinner, from the same payload with no round trip. This is genuinely superior to what commercial trackers ship.

2. **Unit integrity in the composer.** [`AddFood.tsx`](file:///Users/zhakhangiranuarbek/Documents/Projects/diet-app/components/AddFood.tsx) handles the distinction between "per 100g", "as eaten", and "unitless" with mathematical rigour. Switching a unit *restates the amount* rather than reinterpreting the number. Incompatible pairings (g ↔ ml) are refused, never guessed. This prevents the silent data corruption that plagues every mainstream diet app.

3. **The peer-interface contract.** The UI and the AI assistant write to the same records, and the `staleSince` indicator acknowledges that fact on screen. The MCP connector URL is treated as a bearer credential — masked by default, revealed deliberately. This is forward-thinking architecture surfaced as honest UI.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] No onboarding: the second user hits a wall

**What:** There is no first-run flow, no empty-state guidance, no contextual help anywhere in the product. A new user arriving at the Food tab sees a week strip, a zero-calorie readout, empty macro meters, and a collapsed "Add food" panel. Nothing explains what to do first, what a "goal" is in this context, what the copy tray does, or why the quick-add strip is empty.

**Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md confirms the direction is "a handful of invited people later." Those people will not have read the README. The current experience for a second user is: register → see a wall of zeros → tap "Add food" → encounter seven numeric fields and a "portion / per 100g" toggle with no explanation → abandon. The app's entire value proposition — sustained, low-friction logging — collapses if the first session is high-friction.

**Fix:** Design progressive empty states for the first session. When goals are unset, the meter area should say "Set daily goals →" linking to Settings. When the quick-add strip is empty, show "Log your first meal to build your quick-add strip." The AddFood composer needs one line of inline help explaining the portion/per-100g distinction when no food has been selected.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable onboard`

### [P1] Help and documentation is the system's weakest axis (scored 1/4)

**What:** Domain concepts are assumed knowledge throughout. "TDEE" appears with no definition. The difference between "portion", "per", and "unitless" references in AddFood — the conceptual heart of the composer — is invisible to anyone who hasn't read the source code. The API docs page exists but nothing in-app explains the product's own concepts.

**Why it matters:** Even the owner's technically literate friends will not know what TDEE stands for, or why "per 100g" and "as eaten" produce different entries. The product's sophistication becomes its barrier.

**Fix:** Add one-line contextual definitions: "TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) — estimated calories your body uses per day" below the TDEE header in GoalsCard. Add a brief help line beneath the portion/per-100g segmented control in AddFood when no food is selected. These are labels, not documentation — they fit the system's density.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Adherence calendar inaccessible on touch

**What:** In [`TrendsCard.tsx`](file:///Users/zhakhangiranuarbek/Documents/Projects/diet-app/components/TrendsCard.tsx), once the user logs >31 days, the adherence grid folds into a calendar of small squares. The only way to identify which day a square represents is via a native `title` tooltip, which is entirely inaccessible on mobile touch screens — the device the app is designed for.

**Why it matters:** The adherence view is the primary way to see logging consistency over time. On the phone — the app's first-class device — it becomes an unreadable texture past the first month. The data is there; the user cannot reach it.

**Fix:** Add a tap handler that selects a day cell and displays its date and value in a small inline readout below the grid, or use `aria-label` with a visible focus state on each cell. The grid should remain compact, but each cell must be identifiable without hover.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P2] Auth screens lack product voice

**What:** The login and register pages ([`AuthForm.tsx`](file:///Users/zhakhangiranuarbek/Documents/Projects/diet-app/components/AuthForm.tsx)) are a generic centered card with two fields. The copy is adequate ("Review your day or keep logging") but the page has no visual connection to the terminal-readout aesthetic. No monospace numerals, no gridlines, no instrument-panel density. It could belong to any app.

**Why it matters:** The auth screen is the first thing a new or returning user sees. For a product whose entire identity is "an instrument, not a friend," the front door should feel like the instrument, not like a Bootstrap template.

**Fix:** Bring the auth card onto the same visual language: add the `.gridlines` background behind the form (or a subtle version of it), use the system's panel/hairline vocabulary, and set the heading in the headline style (uppercase, tracked). The fields already use `.field`, which is correct.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

### [P3] No keyboard shortcuts for daily navigation

**What:** Navigating between days requires tapping the week strip. There are no keyboard shortcuts for previous day, next day, or "go to today." No shortcut opens Add food or switches tabs.

**Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md identifies desktop as a secondary context for "settings, API key, longer corrections." A desktop user correcting entries across multiple days must click the strip repeatedly. Arrow keys for day navigation and a keyboard shortcut for Add food would make the secondary context significantly more efficient.

**Fix:** Add `←`/`→` arrow key listeners for day navigation (when no input is focused), `t` for today, and a global shortcut (e.g., `n` or `/`) to open/focus Add food.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`
